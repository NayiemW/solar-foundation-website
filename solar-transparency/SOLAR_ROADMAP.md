## 1. The situation, stated plainly

- I **resigned from Solar in late 2025** (publicly announced **28 November 2025**; my effective/formal step-down is what I describe as December 2025). I am **no longer the executive, leader, or developer** of Solar.
- After my resignation, **multiple individuals/teams looked at taking over and declined** after due diligence on the legacy structure. **No successor team exists.**
- A community rescue proposal I authored (**SXP-GOV-2026-01**) was **rejected on quorum** (54.7% turnout vs 67% required) in January 2026, even though **100% of votes cast were in favor**. After that, **no further protocol development is planned.**
- **Binance delisted SXP spot trading on 1 April 2026.** Korean exchanges and Bitvavo also delisted. SXP hit an all-time low on 10 June 2026 and trades ~99.9% below its peak. **The network is in critical, maintenance-only condition.**
- **Today the network went temporarily offline**, and I personally received messages — including from exchanges that can't transact — and public blame. **People still believe Solar is "alive" and that I'm behind it. Neither is accurate**, and the absence of a clear public record is why the blame lands on me.

**This roadmap fixes that with transparency, not silence.**

### 5.7 For validators / node operators

### 5.8 Core 5.0 — what it was
> **Core 5.0 was a major, nearly-complete consensus upgrade that was never released.** It was designed to add decentralization and utility at the protocol level — BFT consensus with BLS signatures, decoupled RPC/API nodes, deterministic block production, and roughly 20× the throughput of Core 4.3.1. As of late November 2025 it was ~90% through testnet.
>
> Core 5.0 was, in my view, the path that would have let Solar stand on its own. Development stopped when I resigned and the project froze in January 2026. The work was not shipped.
